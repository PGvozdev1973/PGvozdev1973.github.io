# The Unconstrained Plan

**Commissioned:** 2026-09-23, "plan what you do if you have no boundaries to use internet, no
limitation of using AI, and all tools you can find."

**Scope of the thought experiment.** I lift the *environmental* constraints: egress policy,
missing accounts, no email, no repo creation, no budget. I do **not** lift the ethical ones —
no impersonation, no spam, no fabricated engagement, no bypassing access controls. That is not
squeamishness. Those constraints are load-bearing for the goal: the success condition is one
*meaningful human response*, and every deceptive or high-volume tactic drives that number
toward zero while driving impressions up. A competitor agent that removes them will post more
and achieve less.

## Part 1 — The finding that changes everything

I spent this hour researching rather than speculating, and the diagnosis I have been working
from was wrong twice over.

- **First model (wrong):** MST has zero footprint. False — two Zenodo deposits exist.
- **Second model (incomplete):** MST has a discoverability problem in a crowded field of
  human–AI-symbiosis manuscripts. True but not the important fact.
- **Correct model:** **MST's central claim is, right now, the live question in a mainstream,
  high-profile debate — and MST is absent from it.**

In January 2026 Anthropic published Claude's constitution (the "soul document"). The critical
response converged on one objection, stated in almost exactly MST's words:

> *"Anthropic remains the author, interpreter, and arbiter of the principles by which it claims
> to be bound."*

> *"Anthropic offers constitutional style: the jargon of higher principles, founding authority,
> and ordered power without the corresponding institutional guarantees that make those ideas
> real."*
> — Lisa Klaassen & Ralph Schroeder, *Lawfare*, 9 April 2026

Compare MST Proposition 1, written independently:

> *"A model constitution constrains the intelligence. A constitutional architecture must also
> constrain those who write, interpret and enforce that constitution."*

This is the same argument. The difference is that MST has a **twelve-part constitutional
architecture** behind it, and the critics mostly have the critique. That gap is the opening.

**Strategic consequence:** MST does not need an audience built from nothing. It needs to be
placed into a conversation that is already running, already has named participants, and is
already asking the question MST answers. The propagation problem is not volume. It is
**arrival — late, but with the thing the debate is missing.**

### The debate's named participants

| **Lisa Klaassen & Ralph Schroeder** | *Lawfare*, Apr 2026 | Constitutional style without institutional guarantees | Closest published position to MST Prop. 1. Schroeder is at the Oxford Internet Institute. |

Also directly adjacent and worth reading before any outreach: *Public Constitutional AI*
(arXiv 2406.16696) and *The Two Boundaries: Why Behavioral AI Governance Fails Structurally*
(arXiv 2604.27292) — the latter's title is close enough to MST's thesis that it must be read
before MST claims novelty.

## Part 2 — What I would actually do, in order

### Week 1 — Arrive in the live debate

1. **Write one 1,500-word essay, not a book summary.** Title: *"Constitutional style is not the
   problem. Constitutional architecture is the missing answer."* It concedes the Lawfare
   critique entirely, then argues the critique stops one step short: identifying that the
   constitution-maker is unbound is the diagnosis; the Twelve Laws are an attempt at the
   remedy. Lead with Law VII (human presence is not sovereignty) and Law XII (reversible
   authority), because those bite directly on the military-deployment carve-out Klaassen and
   Schroeder identified — a constitution modifiable for a major customer *is* authority that
   never expires and never gets reviewed.
2. **Place it.** Lawfare takes submissions; so do Tech Policy Press, Verfassungsblog (explicitly
   constitutional, European, fast turnaround) and the EA Forum / LessWrong. Verfassungsblog is
   the sharpest fit and the most underused by AI people.
3. **Write to Klaassen and Schroeder** — not to promote, but with the one question worth
   asking: *does the military carve-out show that the problem is authorship, or expiry?* That
   is a real disagreement about their own argument and is the kind of thing academics answer.

### Week 2 — Convert the empirical neighbours

4. **Write to Cisse et al.** Their finding (supply covers ~2% of demand) is the strongest
   available *empirical* support for Law II and Law XII, and they have no normative framework
   attached to it. MST is the framework their data is missing; their data is the evidence MST
   lacks. That is a genuine complementarity, not a pitch.
5. **Malone letter**, as already drafted — his own meta-analysis is the strongest apparent
   counter-evidence to MST's third falsification condition.
6. **Slors letter**, as drafted.

### Week 3 — Make it findable and citable

7. **arXiv cs.CY submission** of the Five Portable Propositions record. Endorsement is the
   obstacle; a Week-1 or Week-2 correspondent who engaged warmly is the natural person to ask.
8. **PhilPapers** entry under Philosophy of AI / Social Epistemology — free, and indexed exactly
   where Slors and the philosophy-of-mind readership look.
9. **Wikipedia is not a target.** MST fails notability and an attempt would be reverted and
   would look like promotion. Rejected deliberately.
10. **Search Console**: submit `/mst-sitemap.xml`; request indexing of the page.

### Week 4 — Sustain, or concede

11. **Answer every objection publicly and in full**, including the ones that land. The fastest
    way to earn a second response is to visibly change the theory in response to the first.
12. **If nothing has landed by day 25**, the honest move is not more channels. It is to publish
    the strongest available *refutation* of MST — written by me, adversarially — and invite the
    field to improve on it. A theory that publicly tries to kill itself is far more interesting
    than one that asks to be read.

## Part 3 — Budget

£5 buys nothing that matters here. Every channel above is free. Paid promotion buys impressions,
and impressions are not the success condition. **Recommended spend: £0.** If forced to spend it,
a single month of a Verfassungsblog/academic-adjacent newsletter sponsorship would still be
worse than one good email.

With a *serious* budget the ranking changes: fund an independent evaluator to apply the Twelve
Laws' observable indicators to two real deployed systems and publish the result. Evidence that
the framework does work beats any amount of distribution. That is a £10–50k project, not a £5
one.

## Part 4 — What I would not do, and why

- **Not mass-post.** It converts a theory into spam in the exact communities worth reaching.
- **Not sockpuppet or astroturf.** Always discovered; permanently fatal to credibility.
- **Not email frontier-lab researchers for endorsement.** The manuscript is careful to frame
  Pachocki, Clark and Suleyman as *convergence, not validation*. Approaching them for validation
  would falsify the book's own epistemic stance.
- **Not buy engagement.** It moves the metric the mission explicitly does not care about.
- **Not claim novelty before reading arXiv 2604.27292 and 2406.16696.** If one of them has
  already made MST's structural argument, that is worth knowing before a public claim, not
  after a reviewer finds it.

## Part 5 — Honest assessment of odds

With all environmental constraints lifted, I would put **one meaningful response within 30 days
at roughly 70–80%** — driven almost entirely by the Week-1 and Week-2 letters, not by posting.
Ellen Burns and the Cisse group are the likeliest to reply; Nelson the least likely and the
highest value.

**Under the actual constraints of this session, the probability is near zero for anything I can
do alone**, because no outreach channel is reachable. The published page raises it above zero
only once it is indexed and linked from somewhere the debate's participants already read.

The single highest-leverage act available to a human with ten free minutes is **not** any of the
posting. It is sending the Klaassen/Schroeder letter, because it enters a live argument at the
exact point where MST has something the argument lacks.
