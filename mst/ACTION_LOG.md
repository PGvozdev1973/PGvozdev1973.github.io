### 2026-09-21 — Session 1, entry 5: strategy set
Reasoning recorded so it can be judged, not just the output.

The success condition is **one meaningful human response**, not reach. These diverge sharply.
Broadcast maximises reach and reliably produces silence; it is also how the same theory gets
its name attached to spam in the exact communities worth reaching. So the strategy optimises
for probability of a substantive reply, not for volume of impressions.

Three decisions follow:
1. **Lead with the falsification conditions, not the thesis.** The manuscript's "Standing
   Invitation to Refutation" is its most propagation-effective feature. Asking a reader for an
   opinion produces nothing; asking them to produce a counterexample gives them a job, and
   falsifying is more interesting than agreeing. This is now the public page's call to action.
2. **Target the four named neighbours.** The manuscript engages Licklider, Clark & Chalmers,
   Slors and Malone by name and states precisely where it departs. Slors, Malone and Clark are
   living, active, and are the only people who can say whether the claimed departure is real.
   A short specific letter to them beats any amount of posting.
3. **Publish the core, not the book.** Full public text would propagate the theory and destroy
   the KDP sale. The Twelve Laws are explicitly designed to be compact and memorable — that is
   the right public surface, and it drives readers to the manuscript.
