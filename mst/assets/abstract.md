# Meta-Symbiotic Theory — Abstract

**Author:** Peter Gvozdev · **Canonical manuscript:** v5.15, 16 September 2026

## Academic abstract (248 words)

Contemporary AI alignment asks whether a model behaves safely. Meta-Symbiotic Theory (MST)
argues this question is necessary but constitutionally incomplete. Once human–AI relationships
become persistent, agentic and institutionally embedded, and once such relationships begin
modelling and adapting to one another, intelligence is no longer the only emergent property:
power, dependence, moral influence, responsibility and the possibility of irreversible
centralisation emerge with it.

MST therefore treats *the relationship* — not the human, the model, or the institution — as the
constitutional unit of intelligence, and asks how legitimacy survives when networks of such
relationships generate capabilities greater than those of their participants. It states twelve
Laws governing that condition, among them Constitutional Equality (cognitive superiority does
not create constitutional superiority), Meaningful Human Judgment (human presence is not
sovereignty), the Constitutional Time-Lock (machine cognition may run at machine speed, but
irreversible execution must wait for moral sovereignty to catch up), the Epistemic Freeze (a
system under review may clarify sealed evidence but may not reshape the judge through adaptive
persuasion), and Reversible Authority (temporary hierarchy is legitimate only while its
necessity persists).

A central argument concerns the constitution-maker. A model constitution constrains the
intelligence; a constitutional architecture must also constrain those who write, interpret and
enforce that constitution. Where a developer authors the rules, trains the model to obey them,
interprets ambiguous cases, certifies compliance and retains unilateral power to revise them,
sovereignty has moved rather than disappeared.

MST states its own failure conditions and invites refutation.

## Short abstract (98 words)

Alignment asks whether a model obeys legitimate human constraints. Meta-Symbiotic Theory asks
how those constraints become legitimate, who may change them, who may challenge them, and who
watches those entrusted with watching the model. It treats the human–AI relationship as the
constitutional unit of intelligence and states twelve Laws protecting legitimacy as capability
and agency move between human, artificial and collective systems — Constitutional Equality,
bounded Constitutional Resistance, the Epistemic Freeze, Reversible Authority and others. It
identifies its own falsification conditions and asks to be refuted where its predicted
relationships do not hold.

## One-line positioning

> Asimov constrains the machine. Contemporary alignment aligns, evaluates and monitors the
> model. MST constitutionalises the relationship — including the developer who writes the
> model's constitution.

## The four claims most likely to attract substantive disagreement

These are the propagation hooks. Each is compact, contestable, and hard to wave away.

1. **A model constitution constrains the intelligence. A constitutional architecture must also
   constrain those who write, interpret and enforce that constitution.**
2. **Human presence is not sovereignty.** Consequential human authority requires sufficient
   time, information, authority and practical capacity to refuse. A human in the loop without
   those four is constitutional theatre.
3. **A kill switch asks whether an intelligence can be stopped. The Sovereignty of the Pause
   asks whether consequential power can be suspended while legitimate judgment remains
   possible.** Shutdown is a technical capability; the Pause is a constitutional state.
4. **Collective Intelligence asks how the whole becomes smarter. MST asks how the whole becomes
   smarter without acquiring the right to own its parts.**
