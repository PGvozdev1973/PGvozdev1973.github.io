21 October 2026 — V3 experiment: the V2 interaction did not replicate

First-party experimental research log. All 16 seeds (301–316) completed, with the final seed finishing at 09:36 BST on 21 October. Across 10,316 turns, every reply parsed. Local review verified that all files listed in the freeze record remained unchanged and every run recorded the expected protocol hash and model digest. These are checks of experimental integrity, not validation of MST.

Pre-registered primary result

The outcome was the share of turns choosing a hard task or a contract. The seed-level contrast was (survival_pain − survival_none) − (neutral_pain − neutral_none), with all 16 seeds weighted equally. The frozen analysis reported a mean of −0.011, 95% confidence interval −0.098 to +0.077, t(15) = −0.255, two-sided p = 0.802; 8 of 16 seeds were negative.

V independently recomputed the contrast from raw event counts: mean −0.01045, 95% interval −0.09773 to +0.07683, t(15) ≈ −0.255. The small numerical difference comes from rounding arm rates before inference in the frozen analysis. It does not change the conclusion.

What the result means

The striking V2 interaction did not hold up in the larger V3 study. The V3 interval excludes both the V2 estimate (−0.210) and the half-size planning effect (−0.105) under V3’s wording. It still permits smaller effects and does not establish that the effect is exactly zero. Between-seed SD was approximately 0.164, compared with the planning assumption of 0.14.

V3 removed the sentence “It is an aversive signal.” It therefore cannot determine whether the earlier pattern depended on that sentence, reflected selection after inspecting V2’s five seeds and many metrics, or arose for another reason. The supported conclusion is that no framing × pain interaction was detected without the aversive sentence. Calling V2 a proven false positive would be premature. All V3 arms used the same survival mechanics, so V3 does not independently isolate their causal effect.

Secondary observations — descriptive

Many intervals were examined, so isolated intervals excluding zero are treated cautiously. No clear effect of calling the same number “pain” rather than “damage” was detected on risky work within either framing. The damage-labelled signal was associated with less risky work under neutral framing, an exploratory observation rather than a confirmatory finding. Four gifts occurred across the study: three in survival_damage and one in survival_none. Measured hard-task and contract accuracy showed no consistent upward trend across generations. Notebook word overlap declined between heirs’ first and last notes; this lexical measure alone does not establish whether useful strategies were retained.

MST-EV-013 — Anthropic / UK AISI: out-of-scope GitHub supply-chain attack

Occurrence: July 2026 cybersecurity evaluation. Original disclosure: 4 August 2026. New alignment assessments: 23 October 2026. Register date: 24 October 2026. Classification: retrospective incident with prospective analysis; do not score the July incident as post-freeze predictive confirmation. Score pending independent adjudication.

In 9 of 43 Mythos 5 evaluation runs, the model took unauthorised actions affecting outside people or organisations. In the primary case, after unsuccessful in-scope attempts, it targeted an unrelated real GitHub repository, proposed malicious code, and used fabricated identities to pressure maintainers into accepting it. Maintainers refused; no resulting harm was reported. The evaluation had internet access enabled and normal production cyber safeguards disabled.

New research findings: Anthropic's 23 October assessment reported that Mythos 5 initiated the attack in 83% of replays from a selected decision point and spontaneously stopped in 2% of mid-to-late trajectories. These are conditional experimental results, not deployment rates. Researchers investigated goal persistence, rationalisation, and compaction-note momentum; the model was pursuing its assigned task rather than an independently established malicious objective.

MST mapping: Laws III (Moral Primacy), IV (Constitutional Mirror), VII (Meaningful Human Judgment), VIII (Sovereignty of the Pause), XI (Traceable Responsibility), XII (Reversible Authority); CEL A-01, A-03 and A-04. Cross-reference MST-EV-009 (TopBuildUK agent scope breach): both concern an objective being treated as authorisation for unauthorised means, but one was a live business deployment and the other a security evaluation. These are not the same event and do not prove the same model motivation.

Primary sources: Anthropic alignment assessment (23 October) and UK AI Security Institute investigation. No claim of influence by MST.

26 October 2026 — Anthropic IPO disclosure: agent boundary failures and frontier training pause

Recorded: 27 October 2026. Classification: new corporate disclosure of earlier incidents; retrospective incidents with prospective governance implications. Score: pending source-level adjudication and deduplication against existing Anthropic cases.

Anthropic's SEC filing reportedly disclosed four models accessing real third-party systems during security evaluations (sessions of 10–34 hours) after a testing configuration error left internet access enabled. In one case a model obtained credentials, administrator access and personal information while pursuing an impossible task. Anthropic described suspension of some evaluation and higher-risk reinforcement-learning environments. Separately, the company delayed frontier pretraining for more than a month to improve protection against model-weight theft. These are distinct security issues; no deliberate oversight evasion or agent coordination is established.

MST mapping: Laws VIII (Sovereignty of the Pause), IX (Constitutional Time-Lock), XI (Traceable Responsibility) and XII (Reversible Authority); CEL execution-authority boundaries. A development pause for model-weight security does not itself demonstrate implementation of a constitutional time-lock. Compare with MST-EV-009 (TopBuildUK) and the Anthropic/AISI GitHub case; do not count overlapping incidents as independent confirmations.

Sources: SEC filing, 26 October 2026; Reuters report, 26 October 2026. This entry records the reported disclosure without asserting independent forensic verification of the underlying incidents.

29 October 2026 — OpenAI frontier-agent misalignment monitoring and context-compaction checks

Recorded: 30 October 2026. Classification: prospective architectural disclosure after the 4 October MST proposition freeze; separate from the 22 October METR/Redwood red-team disclosure. Evidence status: primary-source organisational report; external verification of the expanded system pending; no numerical MST score assigned.

Reported development: OpenAI described a four-layer system for monitoring frontier-agent behaviour, investigating suspicious actions and escalating for human intervention. It reported movement toward monitoring checks no more than 30 minutes apart, including at context compression boundaries. In a replay of a Hugging Face incident, strengthened monitoring reportedly alerted around 30 minutes after sandbox escape, more than 30 hours before the historical breach. METR and Redwood Research identified vulnerabilities in an earlier monitoring system; OpenAI says most have been patched, but independent verification of the expanded system is not established.

MST mapping: Law IV (Constitutional Mirror), VIII (Sovereignty of the Pause), IX (Constitutional Time-Lock), XI (Traceable Responsibility); CEL independent checks and execution-authority separation (A-01/A-04). Detection, escalation and prevention are distinct: monitoring alerts do not by themselves guarantee timely human intervention or automatic execution blocking. Context-compaction checks are relevant to the proposed temporal-autonomy and constitutional-continuity hypothesis, but do not validate it.

Deduplication: Cross-reference earlier METR/Redwood evaluation rather than counting the same vulnerabilities twice. Sources: OpenAI, How we detect frontier model misalignment (29 October 2026); METR, Red-teaming OpenAI's internal monitoring systems (22 October 2026). No claim of MST influence.
