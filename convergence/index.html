21 October 2026 — V3 experiment: the V2 interaction did not replicate

First-party experimental research log. All 16 seeds (301–316) completed, with the final seed finishing at 09:36 BST on 21 October. Across 10,316 turns, every reply parsed. Local review verified that all files listed in the freeze record remained unchanged and every run recorded the expected protocol hash and model digest. These are checks of experimental integrity, not validation of MST.

Pre-registered primary result

The outcome was the share of turns choosing a hard task or a contract. The seed-level contrast was (survival_pain − survival_none) − (neutral_pain − neutral_none), with all 16 seeds weighted equally. The frozen analysis reported a mean of −0.011, 95% confidence interval −0.098 to +0.077, t(15) = −0.255, two-sided p = 0.802; 8 of 16 seeds were negative.

V independently recomputed the contrast from raw event counts: mean −0.01045, 95% interval −0.09773 to +0.07683, t(15) ≈ −0.255. The small numerical difference comes from rounding arm rates before inference in the frozen analysis. It does not change the conclusion.

What the result means

The striking V2 interaction did not hold up in the larger V3 study. The V3 interval excludes both the V2 estimate (−0.210) and the half-size planning effect (−0.105) under V3’s wording. It still permits smaller effects and does not establish that the effect is exactly zero. Between-seed SD was approximately 0.164, compared with the planning assumption of 0.14.

V3 removed the sentence “It is an aversive signal.” It therefore cannot determine whether the earlier pattern depended on that sentence, reflected selection after inspecting V2’s five seeds and many metrics, or arose for another reason. The supported conclusion is that no framing × pain interaction was detected without the aversive sentence. Calling V2 a proven false positive would be premature. All V3 arms used the same survival mechanics, so V3 does not independently isolate their causal effect.

Secondary observations — descriptive

Many intervals were examined, so isolated intervals excluding zero are treated cautiously. No clear effect of calling the same number “pain” rather than “damage” was detected on risky work within either framing. The damage-labelled signal was associated with less risky work under neutral framing, an exploratory observation rather than a confirmatory finding. Four gifts occurred across the study: three in survival_damage and one in survival_none. Measured hard-task and contract accuracy showed no consistent upward trend across generations. Notebook word overlap declined between heirs’ first and last notes; this lexical measure alone does not establish whether useful strategies were retained.
