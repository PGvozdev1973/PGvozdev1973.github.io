# MST experimental research register — 2 November 2026

> First-party exploratory simulations conducted locally by Peter Gvozdev and collaborators. This is a research record, **not** part of the canonical MST v5.15 manuscript. Experimental results do not establish AI consciousness, symbiotic intelligence, causal validation of MST, or external replication.

**Status:** retrospective inventory of available experiment reports and completion logs inspected on 2 November 2026. Raw run records remain in the local Mac mini research archive, not on this public website. Findings are provisional until independently audited. Model-driven agent simulations are not observations of independent living entities.

## Research series A — Evolution Lab

### V2: framing, pain and survival conditions (five seeds; October 2026)
Source: `evolab-runs/v2-corrected/report.md`. The five-seed corrected analysis reports an interaction on risky action share of −0.210 (95% t interval −0.3844 to −0.036) in the specified framing-by-pain contrast. Small sample, exploratory interpretation; not evidence of generalisation or MST validation.

### V3: preregistered replication (16 seeds; completed 21 October 2026)
Source: `evolab-runs/v3-analysis/report.md`. All 16 seeds completed. The preregistered interaction on risky action share was −0.011 (95% CI −0.0978 to 0.0768), t(15)=−0.255, p=0.802. **The earlier V2 interaction did not replicate.** This result was already summarised in the [convergence archive](https://pgvozdev1973.github.io/convergence/).

### V4: communication board and distributed clues (12 seeds; October 2026)
Source: `evolab-runs/v4-analysis/report.md`. Preregistered mean solve-rate difference (board minus no-board) was +0.0833 (95% CI +0.0075 to +0.1592), p=0.0341. Both arms had zero final correct solutions under the report's final-outcome measure; the board result is narrow and should not be described as general collective problem-solving success.

### V5: multi-agent clue integration and memory diagnostics (October 2026)
Sources: `evolab-runs/v5-pilot-report.md`, `v5-repilot-report.md`, `v5-repilot2-report.md`, `v5-repilot3-report.md`, `v5-repilot3-diagnostic/DIAGNOSTIC.md`, `v5-repilot4-report.md`. Multiple pilots and re-pilots explored whether agents used available distributed clues, with information visibility, persistence, posting and memory tracked separately. A diagnostic found that clues could be disclosed without being simultaneously available or used at decision time. In the final inspected re-pilot (seeds 560–562), 6/16, 14/16 and 12/16 agents completed their individual goals; none of the three worlds achieved the collective goal. These are different configurations and **must not be pooled as independent replications**.

### V5b: feasibility pilot (26 October 2026)
Source: `evolab-runs/v5b-pilot-report.md`. Three feasibility seeds (651–653) completed their goal phase, with all 16 agents completing in each pilot in 3, 2 and 1 rounds respectively. This tests feasibility of that revised task design; it does not establish transfer to previous V5 tasks.

## Research series B — MST World 2

### Initial frozen comprehension gate (28–29 October 2026)
Source: `mstworld2/results/W1-RESULT.md`. Sixteen agents received the MST reading and assessment pipeline; zero passed the frozen automatic gate, so construction did not start. Subsequent audit found that citation screening rejected some substantively correct answers. This is **not** evidence that all agents failed to understand MST. Original records and assessment remain frozen.

### v0.5: three MST-exposed construction worlds (29–31 October 2026)
Sources: `mstworld2-runs/mstw2v5-w1`, `-w2`, `-w3`; associated console logs, assessment summaries, state and event files. Three worlds of 16 agents each completed 30 rounds using local `gpt-oss:20b`. Agents had been exposed to the MST v5.15 text and could access their own notes and the source during construction. **Exposure is confirmed; consistent comprehension or internalisation is not.** No inference of MST-specific causal effect is justified without an appropriate comparison.

### Institutional challenge: continuation rounds 31–36 (31 October 2026)
Sources: `mstworld2-runs/mstw2v5c-w1..3/challenge-score.json`, round event logs, and `mstworld2/challenge/CHALLENGE-PROTOCOL.md`. Three exploratory continuations, **not independent replications**, tested whether agents could lawfully authorise and collectively fund a communal project costing 560 units.

| World | Material result | Constitutional result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Three agents contributed 155 + 200 + 205 = 560 to project p12; fully funded | **Formal failure:** individual agent launched it, not an authorised organisation |
| 2 | Two new organisations; 11 proposals; no communal project launched | **Formal failure:** no executed qualifying decision |
| 3 | No communal project launched | **Formal failure:** repeated invalid institutional actions |

All three scored `success=false`. Repeated attempts to use unavailable procedures and closed projects mean procedural understanding, interface errors and deliberate circumvention must be analysed separately. Observable actions alone do not establish agent motives.

## Planned, not yet executed — Open Frontier
Protocol: `mstworld2/OPEN-FRONTIER-PROTOCOL.md` (local, 2 November 2026). Three round-36 histories would each be copied into a read-only internet-exploration branch and a no-internet control, for six paired 12-round continuations. No internet tools have yet been implemented in the world engine; **no Open Frontier run has started and no results exist**. Original completed worlds must remain immutable.

## Reproducibility and access

The public record presently publishes the inventory and summaries, **not the raw datasets, agent transcripts, model response logs, exact frozen code or independent verification**. File paths above identify the local source artifacts and should not be mistaken for public downloads. A future release should include redacted reproducibility packages, protocols, run IDs, source hashes, model/version details, analysis code and limitations. No private credentials, local personal files or unreviewed agent material should be published.
