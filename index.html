Meta-Symbiotic Theory

Constitutional Architecture for Relational Intelligence · version 5.15 · Peter Gvozdev

A Symbiotic Mind is not the final stage of intelligence. It is the beginning of another.

Meta-Symbiotic Theory begins where Psychomechanical Symbiosis Theory reaches its natural horizon: when autonomous human–AI relationships cease to exist in isolation and begin adapting to one another. MST studies the intelligence, morality and constitutional order that emerge between those relationships.

The theory in twelve Laws

  1. ICommunication Without Irreversible CentralisationMaximise communication while minimising irreversible centralisation.
  2. IIConstitutional EqualityCognitive superiority does not create constitutional superiority.
  3. IIIMoral PrimacyMoral orientation must precede unrestricted optimisation.
  4. IVThe Constitutional MirrorNo participant or system is exempt from examining what its exercise of power is becoming.
  5. VMoral LineageBefore judging what an artificial intelligence teaches, trace who taught the teacher.
  6. VIConstitutional ResistanceA legitimate artificial participant must retain a bounded, intelligible and proportionate capacity to object.
  7. VIIMeaningful Human JudgmentHuman presence is not sovereignty; consequential human authority requires sufficient time, information, authority and practical capacity to refuse.
  8. VIIIThe Sovereignty of the PauseNo irreversible act is legitimate when meaningful constitutional review has been structurally denied.
  9. IXThe Constitutional Time-LockMachine cognition may continue at machine speed, but irreversible execution must wait when moral sovereignty must catch up.
  10. XThe Epistemic FreezeDuring constitutional review, a system may clarify the sealed evidence but may not reshape the judge through adaptive persuasion.
  11. XITraceable ResponsibilityEmergent or distributed responsibility may be shared, but it must not become untraceable.
  12. XIIReversible AuthorityTemporary hierarchy is legitimate only while its necessity and function persist; authority must expire, decentralise or be legitimately renewed.
